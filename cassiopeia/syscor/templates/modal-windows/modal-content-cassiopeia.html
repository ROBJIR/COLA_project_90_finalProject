Cassiopeia is easy to spot in the night sky thanks to five bright stars that form the letter W. As the constellation appears to turn around Polaris over the course of the night, it can also look like an M. Named after a queen from Greek mythology, Cassiopeia was where Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe observed a supernova in 1572 that was bright enough to see even in daylight. Near the middle star of the W lies a nebula nicknamed the Ghost of Cassiopeia, whose gas glows under the star’s ultraviolet light.
